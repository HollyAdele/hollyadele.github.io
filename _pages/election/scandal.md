### Ohio Nuclear Bribery Scandal

In 1999, the Ohio legislature separated energy generation from energy delivery and introduced competition into the energy market. Then, natural gas companies used fracking to obtain a lot of cheap natural gas. Some nuclear plants suffered with the competiton. Nuclear energy is more climate friendly than coal and natural gas, but less environmentally friendly than solar and wind.

In 2008, Ohio legislature passed a law requiring energy companies to 1. help their consumers be more energy efficient. (They increased consumer bills every month to fund home efficiency upgrades.) 2. Offer a percentage of renewable energy sources. The law also gave money to renewable energy sources. 

These subsides to renewables and competition from fracking put the Perry Nuclear Plant and the Davis-Besse Nuclear Plant in danger of going out of business. The company FirstEnergy owned both. 

So FirstEnergy gave a lot of money to a fake company called Generation Now. Generation Now gave a lot of money to some politicians to get elected and one politician to become Speaker of the House. They were elected and then they passed HB (House Bill) 6. It passed in the Senate and Mike Dewine signed it in 2019. 

The law caused utilities to become more expensive, with the extra money going to those nuclear plants and to coal plants. It took away subsidies for renewable energy. 

Bryan Hambley (who ran for Secretary of State and lost the Democratic Primary election to Allison Russo. He is now supporting Allison Russo in a big way.) told a story of when he was knocking doors. He met a woman whose electricity was turned off because she was on a fixed income, social security, and when electricy became more expensive, she couldn't afford it. 

The Guardian and Vox wrote articles, calling it the worst energy legislation of the century. 

FirstEnergy paid 60 million dollars to Larry Householder, the politician they helped become Speaker of the Ohio House of Representatives. 

Jon Husted said he didn't know about the bribery. He just liked the bill. The one that eliminated subsidies for clean energy and gave subsidies to coal energy. 

I see large amounts of money going from constituents to politicians. I see politicians helping constituents. I see constituents and politicians working together to achieve things. But then someone goes to jail for 20 years, and two other accused people die. 

Digging deeper into the details, it looks like politics as usual. Ever since Citizens United, that let businesses donate to political campaigns, this is what politics looks like. Something is different about this Householder/nuclear bailout thing, but the difference is not all that stark. 

The official difference is that Larry Householder lied about all these contributions, took in more than was legal, and seemed to do a specific task for a specific amount of money. "Quid pro quo." This for that. Like a transaction. 

Quid pro quo doesn't seem all that different from campaign contributions. Campaign contributions are not vague and general support out of the kindness of their hearts. If these big corporations had kind hearts, they would give to the poor and support policies that would give to the poor. 

Citizens United was a Supreme Court case where some businesses claimed that their free speech was limited because they couldn't spend as much money as they wanted presenting political messages. The Supreme Court ruled that businesses do have free speech, and they should be allowed to spend as much money on political messages as they want. They can't coordinate with campaigns, and they have to disclose who spent the money, but the amount of cash they can spend is unlimited. 

 Businesses are not people. They are businesses. They are not alive. They are not citizens. They do not need free speech. This elevates the messages of the rich over the messages of the poor. It reduces the free speech of the poor.
