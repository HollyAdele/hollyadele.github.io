The mayor, Bryan Suddith, appointed a citizen's committee to review the city charter. 

Steven Strain: lawyer. 

Mary Beth Lehman: wealth management marketer. 

Bill Lautar: city councilman, Dor-wood optimist member. 

Al Fullenkamp: Finance and personnel chair of the Board of Trustees for RTA. 

Diane Farrell: TEDxDayton Steering committee member, from Montgomery County Educational Service Center. 

Gwen Owen: member of the City of Kettering Community Relations Board. 

Chuck Havener: Reynolds and Reynolds board member, raises money for Fairmont athletics. 

Matt Wahrhaftig: Nonprofit fundraiser, certified in Ethical Fundraising. "Crypto Dabbler". 

Ron Klein: a doctor on Columbus? I don't know.   

Nick Raines: Director of the Dayton Literary Peace Prize? Manager for Individual Giving at Dayton Philharmonic Orchestra. 

Bethany Roush: Marketing Manager at ACCO Brands. 

So they are not random citizens off the street. They also don't appear overly partisan. 

The committee made a few edits. 

When a new ordinance was passed, presumably by the city council, it used to be published in the newspaper. But no one reads the newspaper, and it's getting expensive to put an ad there. They want to just post the new law on a random website I couldn't find. I think they should print it in the Kettering community magazine or on Facebook or in libraries. I will likely vote against that one. 

City manager and directors now don't have to live in Kettering. If they live here, they have skin in the game. If they don't live here, we can get a wider pool of qualified individuals. I think it's fine if they don't live here; if they are doing a bad job they'll just get fired and we'll find someone else. I'll vote yes on that change. 

People who have a city issue must go before City Council two times and then can go to court. Now, the City Council decides the appeal process which might include a court. City Council has enough to do besides listen to people complain that they want chickens.  I'll vote yes on that change. 

The last is to generalize a mayor's pronouns. It provides for a female mayor. We've already had a female mayor, but the people who wrote the charter never thought we would. I'll vote yes on that one.
