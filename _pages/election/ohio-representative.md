### Rose Lounsbury
### vs. Andrea White
### vs. Chuck Horn

Andrea White approved a budget that lowered the tax rate for businesses and high earning individuals. This is "trickle down economics". The thought is that if businesses and individuals with lots of money keep their money, they will give it to their workers and make new products and grow the economy. However, it just goes to CEOs. In the last few years, after these bills were passed, CEOs made, like, 50% more than past years. And their salaries were already really high.

I have a question about trickle down economics. If it really works the way it promises: everybody being better off, why do all rich people keep lobbying for it? 

I just got a mailer from Andrea White that says she cut taxes so people can keep more of their hard earned money to help make ends meet. I think that the people she cut taxes for-- did not need help making ends meet. She cut taxes for people earning more than $100,000 a year. 

Who should pay for the government? Those who benefit. High wage earners have benefited from universities, infrastructure, laws, and the government investments of the past. They have benefitted much more than those who are working hard and barely getting by.

Chuck Horn was an Ohio Republican Senator. Now he's running for the Ohio House of Representatives at the same time as Rose Lounsbury. I wonder why he would split the Republican vote. Last time Rose Lounsbury ran against Andrea White, she almost won. 

Rose Lounsbury is great. I have heard her speak many times. She has been knocking doors weekly since the beginning of the summer. She used to be a school teacher. I know she cares about education. She is working to restore public funds to public schools. 

Let's talk about vouchers. If a public school is failing, it would be nice to be able to take the money the state is paying for your child to attend that public school and send it with your child to another school that is not failing. The failing school would start to want to get its act together and stop failing. Right?

However, in many cases it's being used so that students don't hear messages parents don't like. I want my children to hear all messages that are true. I have a personal commitment to truth. I want them to hear messages that are true that I don't know yet. I am fine with them hearing messages that are not true, because I teach them how to think critically, examine bias, and interrogate sources. I might choose to believe something that is not true, because it is [useful.](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D86K4XF5?lv=shuf&channelId=500&plpRedirect=mhFallback) But I want to hear truth, and I want my children to hear truth. 

The issue is that the public school still has to spend as much money as before the child left. They have to provide the same services whether there are 30 kids in a class or 25. But they have to do it for $40,000 terless (5 students x $8,000). Money gives space for good curriculum, for modern supplies, for substitutes, for professional development, for field trips, for staff appreciation. I don't believe the failure was ever an issue of caring. It was always an issue of budget. And now that issue of budget is worse. 

Vouchers are probably really nice for the families who get them, but they are a negative for society as a whole. Public tax dollars are supposed to go to society as a whole. Public money is for public schools. 

Rose Lounsbury opposes vouchers and will vote against them. She believes that people who work hard should not go bankrupt if they get sick. "In the wealthiest country in the world, no one should struggle to get basic healthcare." She wants to end gerrymandering, voter purges and suppression tactics that "disproportionately affect voters who happen to be young, poor, disabled, elderly, or people of color." She says that the Republican supermajority is bad for democracy. 

Let's define supermajority. There are 99 members in the Ohio House of Representatives and 33 members in the Ohio senate. Bills usually need just one more than 50% of members voting yes to pass. 50 in the house, 17 in the senate. Then the bill goes to the governor to sign. If they veto it, it does not become law. If Ohio congress has more than 60 yeses in the house and 24 yeses in the senate, it's called a supermajority. If the governor vetoes it, a supermajority can override the veto and it becomes law anyway. 

Republicans in the Ohio legislature currently have a supermajority. The governor does not have the veto power. But only 55% of Ohioans voted for Donald Trump. 55% of Ohioans voted for Trump, but Republicans hold 67% of the congressional seats. Republicans have a supermajority, but they do not deserve the supermajority. 

Trump told Republicans to "find more seats." This is done through gerrymandering. Urban areas usually vote Democrat and rural areas usually vote Republican. So if politicians draw the voting maps to match a large rural area with a part of a city, the rural population's vote will outvote the city's. A Republican gets the seat. Nobody represents the city's needs in congress. This isn't democracy. Republicans have a supermajority because Ohio districts are gerrymandered. The Ohio constitution says that districts need to be contiguous (all touching) and compact. I believe they are all contiguous, but not compact.

Voting for Rose Lounsbury will help break the supermajority and will help restore balance. Russia has a single party system, China has a single party system, North Korea has a single party system. Do we want to become a single party system? We do not. Democrats represent the people who want change. The young, the racial minorities, the poor, the workers, the forward-thinking, the free-thinking. Republicans represent the people who want to keep things the way they are. The old, the racial majority, the rich, the CEOs, the investors, the religious. This is not to say that No Democrats are Religous (I am an example of a religious Democrat). This is also not to say that No Republicans are Poor (poor Republicans voted for Donald Trump). Who is right?

Republicans believe in gun rights, abortion restriction, low taxes, defense spending, reduced oversight, and cultural stability. Democrats believe in gun restriction, abortion rights, higher taxes on the wealthy, welfare and environmental spending, increased oversight, cultural change. Which one is right?

That was a trick question. We need both worldviews. We need strong defense AND strong care for the poor. We need to attract businesses AND fund schools. We need both cultural change AND stability. We need change that is a little faster than some people like, and a little slower than some people like. We need a balance. 

I do not believe that the Ohio Congress is currently balanced. The majority has no need to listen to the minority, and the minority is not being taken care of. I trust Rose Lounsbury to take care of the minority.
